01 / Core Philosophy

The bucket and the channel

A bucket trip produces water in proportion to effort. A channel produces zero water until it connects — then flows whether you're digging or sleeping.

Most productivity systems are bucket optimizers. They reward time invested, tasks completed, habits streaked. They make you a more efficient bucket carrier. DepthFirst is different — it's a channel-digging system. The goal isn't to earn more per hour of effort. The goal is to build the infrastructure through which value flows toward you automatically.

The metaphor is precise. When you carry buckets from the ocean to your farm, output is strictly proportional to effort — work stops, water stops. When you dig a channel from the river to your farm, you can dig for months with seemingly nothing to show for it. Then the channel connects and the farm floods. Work and output are decoupled. That decoupling is wealth.

DepthFirst exists to force you to invest focused attention into channels rather than into buckets, and to keep you alive, motivated, and physically capable while you do it.

Key implication

Channel-digging days often look like nothing happened. The system is designed to reward direction (are you digging toward the channel?) not just throughput (did you work hard?). A busy day of drift is worth less than a focused 90-minute dig block.

02 / The Core Mechanism

Two currencies, one ledger

DepthFirst runs on two currencies that serve different purposes. They are tracked separately, spent separately, and they diagnose different problems when they fall out of balance.

Currency 1
Points

Measures inputs and supports. Points are earned by all focused activity — study blocks, gain actions, extracurriculars. They reward the habits that keep you capable of digging tomorrow.

Points are spent on recovery rewards: leisure, rest, experiences.

High points + zero digs = you're maintaining yourself but the channels aren't deepening.

Currency 2
Digs

Measures channel depth. Digs are earned only during focused work blocks that produce a concrete artifact — something that didn't exist before the session.

Digs are spent on channel investment: tools, courses, resources that make digging easier.

High digs + falling points = you're burning the supports to feed the channels. Unsustainable in 3–4 weeks.

Why two currencies matter

A single-currency system collapses direction and effort into one number. You can trick it: read blog posts, watch tutorials, reorganize your desk — all feel productive and might even earn points. But the channel doesn't get deeper.

With two currencies, the trick fails. Points reward the effort; digs demand the artifact. A week of high points but zero digs is visible immediately in the ledger. So is the inverse burnout pattern. The two numbers together form a diagnostic that a single score never could.

The balance check

A healthy week has both numbers moving. A good week is roughly 150 points and 12 digs. If one runs consistently ahead of the other, something is structurally wrong — either you're drifting (high points, low digs) or burning out (high digs, points falling).

03 / Channel Architecture

What a channel is

A channel is any long-arc pursuit where the relationship between effort and output is non-linear — where sustained investment eventually produces compounding or decoupled returns. Channels have three properties that distinguish them from ordinary goals:

They have breakthrough markers. A channel produces nothing until it connects. You must define in advance what "connected" looks like — a concrete threshold that marks the transition from dig-mode to flow-mode.

They silt if neglected. Half-dug channels refill with debris. Channels need consistent minimum pressure even during periods of low intensity. The system enforces this through a daily floor: at least one full dig per channel on the days you've assigned to it.

They have drift-specific failure modes. Each channel has its own category of activities that feel like digging but don't actually deepen the channel. Identifying and naming these for your specific channels is one of the highest-value exercises in the system.

Drift is the real enemy

The drift columns are the highest-value output of the channel taxonomy exercise. Channels don't fail because you didn't work hard. They fail because effort quietly redirected itself into activities that produce the feeling of progress without the depth of it. Re-read your drift lists weekly.

04 / The Dig Unit

What counts as a dig

The dig is the atomic unit of channel depth. Its definition has two components that are both required:

1 dig = one 90-minute phoneless deep-work block, tagged to a channel, that produces a concrete artifact.

The block without the artifact is half a dig — 0.5 credit. You get recognition for the effort but not for channel depth. Two artifacts in one block is still one dig; the unit is the block-plus-artifact pair, not an artifact count. This prevents artifact-farming and keeps the focus on sustained concentration.

What qualifies as an artifact

An artifact is something that didn't exist before the session. It must be concrete enough to point to. Examples by channel:

Channel Full-dig artifacts (examples)
RAVA Commit pushed to a user-facing path · User conversation logged with ≥3 insights · Distribution experiment defined, launched, or analyzed · Activation metric measured and recorded · Pricing decision documented or tested
SWE Public artifact pushed (commit, release, post) · Article or post published (drafted ≠ shipped) · Specific named outreach sent · Merged OSS PR · Case study or productization piece completed
Trading Trade entered with written pre-reasoning in journal · Backtest run with results documented · Log review session with written observations · Risk-rule update with written rationale · Study session with written takeaways tied to specific instrument/setup

The daily floor

Each day you choose two channels to focus on (rotating across the week so each channel gets 4–5 days of pressure). For each chosen channel, the minimum commitment is one full dig — one 90-minute artifact-producing block. This is the floor. Without it, the channel silts.

The rotation prevents burnout on any single channel while maintaining the minimum consistent pressure each one needs. A channel left entirely alone for more than 4 days gets a silt warning in the Dashboard tab of the ledger.

05 / The Point Economy

How points are earned

Points are earned for any focused, intentional activity. They reward the inputs that sustain you — the physical, mental, and spiritual capacity that makes deep work tomorrow possible. Unlike digs, points don't require an artifact. They reward sustained effort and healthy habit investment.

ActivityBase PointsDigsNotes
Dig block — full artifact (90 min) 10 pts 1.0 Multiplied by active boost stack
Dig block — no artifact (90 min) 5 pts 0.5 Effort credit, half depth
Extracurricular focused block (90 min) 5 pts 0 Non-channel learning, hobbies, skill practice
Gain action (Physical / Mental / Spiritual) 8 pts 0 Also activates 1.5× boost for 24h
Day job hours 0 pts 0 Tracked for visibility only — no inflation
On the day job

Work you're bound to do receives no points — inflation from mandatory activity dilutes the signal. But it's tracked. How much of your weekly capacity goes to employment is invisible unless you measure it, and invisible costs make bad decisions look reasonable.

06 / The Boost System

Physical, mental, and spiritual gain

Boosts exist because the system recognizes that channels are dug by a human being — not a machine. Your physical condition, mental sharpness, and spiritual groundedness are not optional lifestyle features. They're the substrate everything else runs on. The boost system makes investing in these substrates mechanically advantageous.

Category 1
Physical Gain

Any action that elevates physical standing — from exercise to anatomy to appearance. Earns 8 pts + activates the Physical boost.

Category 2
Mental Gain

Any action that elevates mental or verbal aptitude — vocabulary, new skills, deliberate study outside channels. Earns 8 pts + activates the Mental boost.

Category 3
Spiritual Gain

Any action deepening connectedness to Christ — prayer, scripture, worship, fasting. Earns 8 pts + activates the Spiritual boost.

How boosts stack

Each active boost adds 0.5× to the point multiplier on all dig blocks. Boosts stack additively:

1 boost active
1.5×
A single gain action this day or yesterday. Dig block worth 15 pts instead of 10.
2 boosts active
2.0×
Any two of the three categories triggered. Dig block worth 20 pts.
3 boosts active
2.5×
All three categories active. Full-stack. Dig block worth 25 pts — 2.5× the base rate.

The cooldown rule

Each boost category has a 24-hour cooldown per category after activation. Physical gain on Monday locks the Physical boost until Tuesday — the same category cannot be re-activated during that window. This prevents farming a single category repeatedly. To stack at 2.5×, you must invest across all three domains within a 24-hour window.

The Dashboard tab shows live boost status (ACTIVE / READY) and hours since last activation for each category. Check it before logging dig blocks to know your current multiplier.

Design intent

The boost system rewards the integrated human — the version of you that trains, thinks, and prays, not just the version that codes. A full-stack 2.5× day, where you exercised, did deliberate learning, and spent time with scripture before a deep work session, is the system firing on all cylinders. Those days exist. The system makes them mechanically superior to push you toward creating them.

07 / The Operational Rhythm

Daily, weekly, monthly

The system runs at three cadences. Each serves a different function. Getting all three right is more important than optimizing any one of them.

Daily
Capture & execute

Log every event in the Daily Log as it happens. Pick two channels for today. Hit the floor (one full dig per channel). Check the Dashboard before and after work sessions.

Weekly (Sunday)
Review & reorient

30–45 minutes. For each channel: did it deepen? What was the drift leak? What's the one highest-leverage dig next week? Check point/dig balance. Decide reward spends.

Monthly
Metrics & markers

Update leading and lagging metrics per channel. Check progress toward breakthrough markers. Look at drift patterns across the month — not just this week. Adjust the system only after month four.

The three weekly review questions

For each channel, every Sunday, answer these three questions in the ledger:

  1. Did the channel get deeper this week? Name a concrete artifact-level piece of evidence, not a feeling.
  2. What activity was high-input but low-depth? Find the leak. It's always there.
  3. What is the one highest-leverage dig for next week? Singular. If you can't name it, you haven't thought hard enough.

These three questions are where the system earns its keep. Daily logging captures data. Weekly review extracts intelligence from it.

08 / The Ledger

The tracking spreadsheet

The DepthFirst ledger is a Google Sheets workbook (imported from the .xlsx template). It has seven tabs, each with a distinct function. The design philosophy: near-zero friction for daily capture, rich structure for weekly review.

01
Dashboard
Live view — check this daily. Shows current point and dig balances, this week's totals per channel, active boost status, silt warnings (red if a channel hasn't been dug in 4+ days), and today's floor check. Updates automatically from Daily Log and Rewards.
02
Daily Log
One row per event. Columns: date, time, type, channel, duration, artifact (yes/half/no), boost category, boost multiplier, notes. Base points, final points, and digs are calculated automatically — you only enter the multiplier and the notes. Dropdown menus on all categorical fields. Supports 200 rows (extend by copying the last formula row).
03
Artifacts
One row per substantial artifact. This is your portfolio. Columns: date, channel, artifact type, description, link, notes. Look back through this quarterly — it calibrates your sense of progress and feeds the SWE channel directly as a credibility record.
04
Weekly Review
Templated, one section per channel plus cross-cutting questions. Auto-pulls this week's dig and point totals. The three review questions per channel are filled manually. This is where intelligence is extracted from raw log data.
05
Metrics
Leading metrics (weekly) and lagging metrics (monthly) per channel, plus breakthrough markers. Updated manually monthly. Leading metrics tell you whether you're digging; lagging metrics tell you whether the channel is responding. Breakthrough markers are pre-defined — when hit, the system shifts mode for that channel.
06
Rewards
Reward menu with prices in points, digs, or both — plus a spend log. Dashboard balances update automatically when a spend row is added. The reward economy is part of the system, not optional extra — spending earned currency is what keeps the engine running.
07
Reference
The system manual — point values, boost mechanics, full dig-vs-drift taxonomy per channel, weekly yield expectations. Read during setup; re-read when behavior starts to drift. This tab is the ground truth that governs the Daily Log.
One rule for the Daily Log

Log events as they happen, not at the end of the day. End-of-day reconstruction is 40% fiction. The 20 seconds of live logging buys you accurate data that actually teaches you something on Sunday.

09 / The Reward Economy

What your currencies buy

Rewards are split into three categories, each priced in the currency most aligned with what earned it. The framing matters: these are not permissions to stop. They are targeted investments in the thing that makes continued digging possible — your recovery, your tools, and your experience of being alive.

Recovery rewards Priced in Points only
Movie or show evening (2h) 30 pts
Eat out at a favourite spot 50 pts
Half-day off-grid (no work) 100 pts
Full Sunday off (no tracking, complete rest) 150 pts
Channel investment Priced in Digs only
Small tool, SaaS subscription, or book (≤$15) 5 digs
Course or paid resource ($30–50) 15 digs
Significant tool or equipment ($100+) 30 digs
Experience rewards Priced in both (balanced output required)
Day trip or local adventure 100 pts + 20 digs
Weekend retreat 200 pts + 40 digs
Bigger trip or significant event 500 pts + 100 digs

Recovery is priced in points because it's earned by sustaining the supports. Investment is priced in digs because channel resources should only be unlocked by channel depth — buying a $50 course with 15 digs means you've logged 22.5 hours of artifact-producing work this channel. Experiences require both, because they're only justified when you're neither burnt out nor drifting.

Don't hoard

A balance of 800 unspent points is a system failure, not a success. Unredeemed rewards signal either that the reward menu doesn't match what you actually want (fix the menu) or that you've detached from the system's human purpose. Spend regularly. The recovery is part of the output.

10 / Versatility

Adapting DepthFirst to any life

The reference implementation uses three specific channels in one specific context. The underlying system is architecture-agnostic. Any life pursuit that has non-linear output dynamics — where sustained investment eventually decouples effort from returns — can be modeled as a channel within DepthFirst.

What needs to be defined for each channel

When adapting DepthFirst to a new context, you need to specify six things per channel. Everything else (the point economy, boosts, ledger structure, rhythm) stays constant.

ElementWhat to define
Channel definition One sentence: what is this, and what does "flowing" look like when the channel connects?
Dig activities 5–8 specific activities that produce artifacts and genuinely deepen this channel
Drift activities 4–6 activities that feel like channel work but don't deepen it — named specifically
Leading metrics Weekly activity-level counts you can control (e.g. conversations logged, features shipped)
Lagging metrics Monthly outcome-level indicators that tell you if the channel is responding
Breakthrough markers 2–4 concrete thresholds that mark the transition from dig-mode to flow-mode

Example channel substitutions

Creative career (writing, music, film)
Channel: a body of published work in a specific niche with a growing audience. Dig artifacts: published pieces, finished tracks, submitted scripts, portfolio items, audience-building experiments. Drift: consuming other creators' content, working on drafts that never get published, endlessly refining unpublished work.
Academic or research track
Channel: published research in a defensible specialty that generates citation, collaboration, and opportunity. Dig artifacts: submitted papers, completed experiments, literature syntheses, conference submissions. Drift: reading without writing, attending seminars without producing output, generic grant browsing.
Physical transformation
This is better modeled as a gain category (Physical Gain) than as a channel, because the output is proportional to input — it doesn't have a breakthrough-decoupling dynamic. Track as a boost trigger rather than a separate channel.
Relationship or network building
Channel: a set of relationships in a specific domain where your name is known and trusted, producing compounding introductions and collaboration. Dig artifacts: specific conversations had, specific people introduced, events organized, follow-throughs logged. Drift: passive social media consumption, attending events without specific targets, general "networking".
One design constraint

Limit active channels to 3, maximum 4. Beyond that, no channel gets enough consistent pressure and all of them silt. The impulse to add more channels — a fifth book project, a new business idea — should be met with a question: which existing channel would this replace? If none, it waits.

11 / Getting Started

Zero to first dig in one week

Download and import the ledger

depthfirst_template_ledger.xlsx 8 tabs · 665 formulas · dropdowns · boost tracker · setup guide
Download

In Google Drive: New → File Upload → pick the file → right-click it → Open with Google Sheets → File → Save as Google Sheets. That last step converts it from an Excel view to a native Sheet — all formulas, dropdowns, and conditional formatting carry over cleanly.

Define your channels

Name 2–3 channels. For each, write one sentence defining what "flowing" looks like. This forces clarity on whether you're actually describing a channel (non-linear output dynamics) or a bucket (proportional effort-to-output). If it's a bucket, it doesn't belong as a channel — it might belong as a gain category instead.

Build your channel taxonomies

For each channel, list your specific dig activities and drift activities. The drift list is more important. Be brutally honest — name the activities you've been calling "work" that don't actually deepen the channel. Update the Reference tab with your taxonomy, and delete the examples if they don't apply.

Set your breakthrough markers

In the Metrics tab, replace the default breakthrough markers with your own. These need to be yours — specific numbers you've chosen, not defaults. They don't need to be conservative. The function is to give you a target that tells you when dig-mode should transition to flow-mode. If you can't define it, you can't recognize it.

Build your reward menu

In the Rewards tab, personalize the menu. The category structure (Recovery / Investment / Experience) and pricing split (Points / Digs / Both) should stay. The specific items should be things you actually want. A reward menu populated with things you don't care about doesn't motivate anything.

Log your first week

Don't optimize in week one. Just log. Every event, as it happens. The goal of the first week is to build the logging habit and calibrate your gut feel for what a day of real digging looks like versus a day of drift dressed as effort. Resist any urge to change the point values or reward prices during this period.

Do your first weekly review (Day 7)

Open the Weekly Review tab Sunday evening. Fill in the three questions per channel from the actual log data, not from memory. Look at the dig/point balance. Look for the drift leak — it's there. Set next week's highest-leverage dig for each channel. This is the most important 30 minutes of the week.

Tune at week four, not before

After four real weeks of logged data, you'll know whether the point values, reward prices, and channel floors are calibrated for your actual life. Before that, you're optimizing a model you don't understand yet. Make adjustments at week four with real data. Any tuning before that is speculation.

12 / Glossary

Terms

CHANNEL
A long-arc pursuit with non-linear output dynamics. Requires sustained investment to connect; produces compounding returns once it does. Has a breakthrough marker that defines the transition from dig-mode to flow-mode.
DIG
The atomic unit of channel depth. One 90-minute phoneless deep-work block, tagged to a channel, that produces a concrete artifact. Worth 1.0 digs if an artifact is produced, 0.5 digs if not.
ARTIFACT
A concrete output from a dig block that didn't exist before the session. Required to earn full (1.0) dig credit. Examples: a commit, a published post, a logged trade with pre-reasoning, a user conversation with documented insights.
DRIFT
Activity that produces the feeling of channel progress without actually deepening the channel. Named specifically per channel in the Reference tab. The most dangerous category because it's both comfortable and convincing.
POINTS
Currency 1. Earned by all focused activity (dig blocks, gain actions, extracurriculars). Measures inputs and supports. Spent on recovery rewards. Low points signal that the substrate enabling channel work is deteriorating.
DIGS
Currency 2. Earned only by artifact-producing dig blocks. Measures channel depth. Spent on channel investment rewards. Low digs signal that effort isn't converting to depth.
BOOST
A 24-hour multiplier on points earned from dig blocks. Activated by a gain action in one of three categories (Physical, Mental, Spiritual). Stacks additively: 1 boost = 1.5×, 2 = 2.0×, 3 = 2.5×. Each category has a 24-hour cooldown after activation.
GAIN ACTION
Any deliberate action that elevates physical, mental, or spiritual standing. Earns 8 base points and activates the corresponding boost category.
SILT WARNING
Dashboard alert triggered when a channel has not been dug in more than 4 days. Named after the physical phenomenon of half-dug channels refilling with debris when pressure is removed.
BREAKTHROUGH MARKER
A pre-defined threshold that marks the transition from dig-mode to flow-mode for a specific channel. Must be defined before tracking begins so you can recognize when the channel has connected. Without breakthrough markers, the dig phase has no end condition.
FLOOR
The minimum daily dig commitment for a chosen channel on days it's been assigned. One full dig (90 min, artifact required). Below this, the channel silts. The floor is non-negotiable during active dig-mode.
DAILY LOG
The primary data entry tab in the ledger. One row per event. The ground truth of the entire system — all Dashboard numbers and Weekly Review data derive from it. Should be updated live, not reconstructed at end of day.
